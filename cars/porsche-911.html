    Dossier Nº 02 / 05911 Carrera 4S

    The idea has not changed. Everything around it has.

    An engine behind the rear axle, two seats and two more for bags, and since 1963 the same stubborn silhouette. The Carrera 4S is the 992 generation with the all-wheel-drive grip of the 4 and the stronger engine of the S.

    Few cars have been argued about for as long as the 911. Its layout, a flat-six hung behind the rear axle, is the kind of thing an engineer would be tempted to abandon. Porsche has spent decades refining it instead. The 992, the eighth generation, was introduced in late 2018 and is the car on this page.

    The name of this version is a recipe. The S means the stronger of the two Carrera engines: 331 kW (450 PS), 22 kW (30 PS) more than the generation before. The 4 means all-wheel drive, with torque sent to the front axle when the rear wheels run short of grip. Everything else is the 911: a low nose, a steep windscreen, and a roofline that falls away toward the tail.

    Read on in the order a driver meets it: the engine behind you, the numbers, the shape, then the road.

    02Engine and performance

    Six cylinders, laid flat, hung behind the axle.

    The engine is a 3.0-litre flat-six: six cylinders in two horizontally opposed banks of three, with a turbocharger feeding each bank. It sits behind the rear axle, in the very tail of the car. That one decision explains most of what a 911 is: the traction it finds leaving a corner, and the care its chassis takes over the weight at its back.

    In the 4S it makes 331 kW (450 PS) at 6,500 rpm and 530 Nm from 2,300 to 5,000 rpm. That is 22 kW (30 PS) and 30 Nm more than the car it replaced, and the same output as the rear-drive Carrera S. The engine turns to 7,500 rpm, and a gasoline particulate filter, new with the 992, cleans its exhaust.

    Power goes through a new eight-speed PDK dual-clutch gearbox, the first eight-speed PDK in a Porsche sports car, and on to all four wheels through Porsche Traction Management. By Porsche’s figures that is 0–100 km/h in 3.6 seconds, 3.4 with Sport Plus selected, and 306 km/h flat out.

    04Exterior and engineering

    An icon, re-engineered.

    The silhouette is the brand, so the 992 keeps it. What changed is how the car is built and what its surfaces do: five details, each with a job.

    1. 01One wide body
    2. 02Aluminium and steel
    3. 03Handles that retreat
    4. 04A tail of one light
    5. 05Surfaces that move
    1. 01

      One wide body for every Carrera

      The 992 is 45 mm wider across the front than the generation before it, and every Carrera, rear-drive or all-wheel drive, now has the wide rear that earlier cars kept for the 4 models. The result is the broad, planted look of a 4S on every 911, and room under the arches for tyres of different sizes: 245s at the front, 305s at the rear on 21-inch wheels.

      Width 1,852 mm excluding mirrors Varies

    2. 02

      Aluminium and steel, together To confirm

      Porsche describes the 992’s body as an aluminium and steel composite built for lightness. The doors and front wings are aluminium, and so is the large outer panel running from the A-pillars toward the rear, which earlier cars made in steel. The structure is stiff where the car needs it and light where it does not. The panel-by-panel detail is the one item on this page not yet checked against Porsche’s current documentation.

      Structure Aluminium and steel composite

    3. 03

      Door handles that retreat

      The door handles are electrically deployed. They present themselves as the driver approaches and lie flush with the body when not in use, which keeps the flank of the 911 clean and its airflow undisturbed. It is a small change, and the one most people notice first.

      Handles Retractable, flush when closed

    4. 04

      A tail of one light

      Across the back, a single full-width light strip joins the two tail lamps, with the PORSCHE lettering set into it. Above the engine, the rear lid is slatted. Seen from behind at night, the 992 is unmistakable before you can read the badge.

      Rear Full-width light strip with integrated PORSCHE lettering

    5. 05

      Surfaces that move

      The side air intakes at the front carry active cooling-air flaps, open when the engine asks for air and closed when it does not. At the back, the spoiler lies in the engine lid and rises automatically as speed builds, adding downforce over the axle that carries the engine. A car this compact does its aerodynamic work with panels that change shape rather than with parts bolted on.

      Aerodynamics Active cooling-air flaps; auto-deploying rear spoiler; 0.29 cd Varies

    05Driving character

    The engine is behind you. That changes everything.

    Driving a 911 starts with where the weight is. With the engine behind the rear axle, the rear tyres carry a large share of the car’s mass and use it to put power down. That is why a 911 can get back on the throttle so early out of a slow corner, and why its rear tyres are so much wider than the fronts.

    All-wheel drive changes the picture only in degree. Porsche Traction Management sends drive to the front wheels when it is needed, while the rear axle still does the main work. The steering stays light and direct, and the car keeps the sense of a tail that is always part of the conversation.

    The 4S weighs 1,565 kg by Porsche’s DIN figure, which is not light for a car this small. The 3.0-litre engine and the eight-speed PDK are what make it quick regardless: the gearbox changes without lifting power, so the 530 Nm is never far from the wheels.

    On cars with the Sport Chrono package, a mode switch on the steering wheel changes the character of the engine, gearbox and chassis in one move. Left in Normal, the 4S is a comfortable grand tourer with a boot in the front. Turned up, it becomes the car its numbers describe.

    06Closing

    Still the 911. Only more so.

    You can park it, drive it to work, and fit a weekend’s bags behind the front seats. And when the road opens up, 530 Nm from 2,300 rpm and four driven wheels turn a long-standing idea into a very current car.
