    Dossier Nº 01 / 05M5 F90 Competition

    Sensible shape. Unreasonable output.

    Four doors, five seats, a boot for the luggage, and under the bonnet a twin-turbocharged V8 tuned to 625 PS. The Competition is the F90 M5 with the last degrees of restraint taken out.

    Every M5 has been built on one proposition: take a business saloon and give it the performance of a sports car, without asking its passengers to give anything up. The F90, the sixth generation, keeps that proposition and adds something the line had not offered before, all-wheel drive.

    The Competition is the sharper reading of the same car. It makes 25 PS more than the standard M5, sits 7 mm lower on springs that are 10 per cent stiffer, and wears its details in high-gloss black. It is not a different car. It is the same car, edited.

    Read what follows in the order a driver meets it: the engine, the numbers, the shape, then the road.

    02Engine and performance

    Eight cylinders, two turbochargers, one wide plateau.

    The engine is the S63: a 4.4-litre V8 with both turbochargers mounted in the V between its cylinder banks. Placing them there keeps the exhaust paths short, which helps the engine answer the throttle quickly, and it keeps the whole unit compact.

    In the Competition it makes 460 kW (625 PS) at 6,000 rpm, and 750 Nm from 1,800 all the way to 5,800 rpm. That torque band is 200 rpm wider than the standard M5’s, so the push stays on for longer as the revs climb.

    Power then passes through an eight-speed M Steptronic automatic with a torque converter, and on to M xDrive: all four wheels when grip is the priority, the rear pair alone when the driver asks.

    04Exterior and engineering

    Restraint, engineered.

    The Competition does not shout. Its changes live in black trim, a lower stance and the details below, each one doing a job.

    2. 02A lighter skin
    3. 03Forged twenties

    1. 01

      Gloss black, applied with discipline

      On the Competition the kidney grille surround, the gill mesh, the mirror caps, the rear bumper trim and the rear spoiler are finished in high-gloss black, and the boot carries its M5 Competition lettering in the same finish. The door openers stay in body colour, so the black reads as a decision rather than a coating.

      Finish High-gloss black, BMW Individual Shadow Line

    2. 02

      A lighter skin To confirm

      BMW builds the M5’s roof from carbon-fibre reinforced plastic and its bonnet and front wings from aluminium, taking weight out of the highest and most forward parts of the car, where it matters most to how it turns in. This is the one detail on this page not yet checked against the maker’s current documentation.

      Materials Carbon-fibre roof; aluminium bonnet and front wings

    3. 03

      Twenty inches, forged

      The Competition stands on 20-inch forged wheels in a Y-spoke design with a bicolour finish. Tyres are staggered, wider at the rear. Behind them sit M Compound brakes with blue callipers as standard; high-gloss black and red are alternatives.

      Tyres 275/35 R20 front, 285/35 R20 rear Varies

    4. 04

      A lower, tighter stance

      The Competition sits 7 mm lower than the M5 and carries more front camber. Springs are 10 per cent stiffer at both ends, the rear anti-roll bar is firmer, the rear toe links use ball joints in place of rubber mounts, and even the engine mounts are stiffer. The car does not look different from a few metres away. It behaves differently from the first corner.

      Against the M5 −7 mm ride height; springs +10 %

    5. 05

      An exhaust with flaps

      The M Sport exhaust is flap-controlled and ends in black chrome tailpipe embellishers. The flaps open and close to change how much the system says, so the same car can pass a quiet street and announce itself on an open one.

      System M Sport exhaust, flap-controlled

    05Driving character

    Four doors, five seats, and 625 horsepower.

    Driven gently, the Competition behaves like the large, quiet saloon it appears to be. Dampers in Comfort, the gearbox shifting early, all four wheels driven: it asks nothing of its passengers.

    The character changes when the driver asks. Dampers, gearbox and drive each have settings of their own, and they can be combined, so the same car can be set up for a wet motorway or for a dry road with its rear axle leading.

    Rear-drive mode is the old M5 in all but name. Engine and gearbox send everything to the rear wheels, and the driver must switch the stability control off to ask for it.

    It is a heavy car, about 1.95 tonnes, and the Competition’s chassis work is aimed squarely at that: stiffer springs, firmer engine mounts and a rear axle with less rubber in it keep the mass from wandering when the road asks questions.

    06Closing

    The ordinary silhouette is the point.

    You can carry four friends and their bags. You can drive it to work in the rain. And when the road opens up, 750 Nm from 1,800 rpm is already there, waiting to be asked.
