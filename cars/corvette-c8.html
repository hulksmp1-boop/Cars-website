    Dossier Nº 05 / 05Corvette C8 Stingray

    Sixty-six years in front, then the engine moved back.

    A 6.2-litre V8 behind the two seats, rear-wheel drive and one gearbox: an eight-speed dual-clutch automatic. This page covers the 2020 model year Corvette Stingray coupe, the first production mid-engine Corvette, with the figures Chevrolet published for that year.

    The Corvette arrived in 1953 as a two-seat American sports car, and for the seven generations that followed, C1 to C7, its engine sat ahead of the driver. Mid-engine Corvettes were tried again and again, but only as research cars and concepts: the CERV I of 1959 and the Corvette Indy of 1986 are two of them. Zora Arkus-Duntov, the engineer long associated with the car, was a persistent champion of the idea.

    The eighth generation is where it reached production. Chevrolet announced the C8 in April 2019 and unveiled the coupe on 18 July 2019 in Tustin, California, as a 2020 model. Production at the Bowling Green Assembly plant in Kentucky officially began on 3 February 2020, after the delay caused by the 2019 General Motors strike.

    Chevrolet called it the first-ever production mid-engine Corvette, and its executive chief engineer, Tadge Juechter, introduced it at the reveal. The car is a 2020 Stingray with the 6.2-litre LT2 engine and the dual-clutch gearbox; later C8 models, the Z06, E-Ray and ZR1, are different cars and are not described here.

    Read on in the order the car is built: the engine, the numbers, the shape, the drive, then the sheet.

    02LT2 V8 engine and performance

    Eight cylinders, two valves each, no turbo.

    The LT2 is a 6.2-litre, 90-degree V8 with pushrod, overhead-valve gear and two valves per cylinder, sixteen in all. It is naturally aspirated, uses direct injection and variable valve timing, and can shut down cylinders under light load through Active Fuel Management. It sits longitudinally behind the cabin, ahead of the rear axle.

    With the performance exhaust, Chevrolet rates it at 495 hp (369 kW) at 6,450 rpm and 470 lb-ft (637 Nm) at 5,150 rpm. Without it the figures are 490 hp and 465 lb-ft. Because the car now corners with the engine behind the driver, the oil system is a dry-sump design, which keeps the oil supply steady in hard cornering.

    Power goes to the rear wheels through a Tremec eight-speed dual-clutch transmission with paddle shifters, the only gearbox offered: there is no clutch pedal, no manual option and no conventional shift lever. Chevrolet quotes 2.9 seconds from 0 to 60 mph for the Z51 coupe and 11.2 seconds through the quarter-mile at 121 mph, with a top speed of 194 mph (312 km/h).

    04Exterior and mid-engine engineering

    The cabin moved forward, the shape followed.

    Putting the V8 behind the seats rewrote the Corvette’s proportions. Five details, each tied to a number or a job.

    2. 02Rear weight bias
    3. 03The aluminium spaceframe
    4. 04Two luggage spaces
    5. 05Brakes and tyres
    1. 01

      Proportions first

      The Stingray is 4,630 mm long on a 2,722 mm wheelbase, 1,934 mm wide and 1,234 mm tall. With the engine behind the cabin, the driver sits closer to the front axle, which Chevrolet described as almost on top of the front wheels, and the long bonnet of earlier Corvettes gives way to a short nose and a cabin set well forward.

      Stance 2,722 mm wheelbase; 1,648 / 1,586 mm track front and rear

    2. 02

      Rear weight bias

      Chevrolet says the layout moves the weight distribution to 40 percent on the front axle and 60 percent on the rear, and that the rear bias helps both in a straight line and on the track. Treat 40:60 as the stated design figure; actual distribution changes with the car as specified and loaded.

      Balance 40 : 60 front to rear (Chevrolet’s stated figure)

    3. 03

      The aluminium spaceframe

      As with the C7 before it, the C8’s structure is a welded aluminium spaceframe, built at the Bowling Green plant. The roof is a removable panel on the coupe, which keeps the open-air Corvette idea within a fixed body.

      Structure Welded aluminium spaceframe; removable roof panel

    4. 04

      Two luggage spaces

      Moving the engine back freed the nose, so the car has a front luggage space as well as one behind the engine. Chevrolet quotes a combined 12.6 cubic feet, or 357 litres, which is the total for both and is limited by load and weight distribution.

      Luggage 357 L (12.6 cu ft), front and rear together

    5. 05

      Brakes and tyres

      Brembo supplies four-piston fixed callipers on vented rotors. The wheels are staggered: 19-inch at the front with 245/35 tyres, 20-inch at the rear with much wider 305/30 tyres, so the driven axle carries the bigger contact patch and most of the car’s weight.

      Tyres 245/35 ZR19 front, 305/30 ZR20 rear

    05Driving character

    The driver forward, the engine behind. Sixty percent of the weight on the rear axle.

    The C8 is a two-seat, mid-engine, rear-drive coupe, and its character comes from where things sit. The cabin is far forward, the V8 is just behind the seats, and the drive goes only to the rear wheels. Chevrolet’s stated aims were better weight distribution and a better sense of control, with the driver positioned nearer the front axle. How that feels from the seat is a matter for the driver’s own judgement; the figures on this page cannot show it.

    The engine is the part that is deliberately familiar. A naturally aspirated V8 with pushrods has no turbocharger to wait for, and its quoted peaks, 470 lb-ft at 5,150 rpm and 495 hp at 6,450 rpm, sit high in the rev range. Cylinder deactivation lets it run on fewer cylinders at light load, and the dry-sump oil system is there for sustained cornering.

    The gearbox is the part that is new. The dual-clutch automatic has no clutch pedal and no lever, only paddles on the wheel, and it is the only transmission offered. Chevrolet quotes 2.9 seconds from 0 to 60 mph for the Z51 car, with the performance exhaust and the dual-clutch gearbox.

    The 3D scene above uses a third-party model, credited in the page footer. It is a stand-in rather than a scan: it is about 13 percent longer and about 9 percent taller than the published dimensions, and its wheels, trim and details are not matched to a specific package.

    07Closing

    Sixty-six years from the first car. One V8, behind the seats.

    The Corvette began in 1953 with its engine in front, and it stayed there through seven generations. The C8 Stingray, unveiled in 2019, kept the V8 and the rear drive and changed everything around them: the cabin, the proportions, the gearbox and the balance. The car that carries the name now is the one on this site with its engine behind the driver.
