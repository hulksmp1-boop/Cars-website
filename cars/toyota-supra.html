    Dossier Nº 04 / 05GR Supra MK5

    A name from 1978, back in a short, wide coupé.

    A turbocharged inline-six, rear-wheel drive and two seats. The fifth-generation Supra returned in 2019 after a long absence, built in Austria and developed with BMW. This page covers the GR Supra 3.0 in its European specification.

    The Supra began in 1978 as a six-cylinder version of the Celica, sold in Japan as the Celica XX. It grew into a model of its own, and its fourth generation, the A80 of 1993, is the one many people remember, with a twin-turbo 3.0-litre inline-six. Japanese production of the A80 ended in August 2002, and the name went quiet.

    It came back in January 2019, when Toyota showed the new car at the Detroit Auto Show. The chassis code, A90, simply continued the sequence of earlier Supras (A40, A60, A70, A80). Toyota presented it as the first global model of its GAZOO Racing performance line.

    The car was developed jointly with BMW and is assembled by Magna Steyr in Graz, Austria, where BMW’s Z4 is also built. Toyota’s chief engineer on the project was Tetsuya Tada. Toyota has said the new car’s design draws on the A80 and on the 1967 Toyota 2000 GT.

    Read on in the order the car is built: the engine, the numbers, the shape, the drive, then the sheet.

    02B58 engine and performance

    Six cylinders, in a row, one turbo.

    The 3.0-litre engine is a turbocharged inline-six: six cylinders in a single line, front-mounted, with a single twin-scroll turbocharger, direct injection, a 24-valve head with two overhead camshafts, and variable valve timing on both camshafts. Toyota’s documents list it under the code B58B30M1; the B58 is a BMW engine family.

    In the European GR Supra 3.0 it makes 250 kW (340 PS) between 5,000 and 6,500 rpm, and 500 Nm of torque from 1,600 to 4,500 rpm. That wide, low torque band means the pull is there for most of everyday driving rather than at one narrow peak.

    Power goes only to the rear wheels, through an eight-speed automatic or a six-speed manual. Toyota Europe quotes 4.3 seconds from 0 to 100 km/h for the automatic and 4.6 for the manual, and an electronically limited top speed of 250 km/h. Toyota also sells a 2.0-litre four-cylinder Supra, which is not the car described here.

    04Exterior and engineering

    Small on purpose, wide where it counts.

    Toyota’s own name for the look was “Condensed Extreme”. Five details, each tied to a number or a job.

    1. 01Proportions
    2. 02The double-bubble roof
    3. 03Balance by design
    4. 04The active differential
    5. 05Brakes and tyres
    1. 01

      Proportions first

      The Supra is 4,379 mm long on a 2,470 mm wheelbase, 1,854 mm wide and only 1,292 mm tall. Its track is 1,594 mm at the front and 1,589 mm at the rear. Divide the wheelbase into the front track and you get a ratio of about 1.55 to 1, the kind of short, wide stance Toyota says it was aiming for, because a short wheelbase turns quickly and a wide track steadies the car.

      Stance 2,470 mm wheelbase; 1,594 / 1,589 mm track; about 1.55 : 1 (calculated)

    2. 02

      The double-bubble roof

      The roof rises in two shallow domes, one over each seat. Toyota says the shape recalls the 1967 Toyota 2000 GT, one of the two older cars it names as the new Supra’s design ancestors, the other being the A80 of 1993 to 2002.

      Heritage Double-bubble roof, after the 2000 GT

    3. 03

      Balance by design

      Toyota lists three aims for the structure: high rigidity, a low centre of gravity and a 50:50 split of weight between the front and rear axles. It also says the styling makes full use of the sports-car packaging. Treat 50:50 as the design target Toyota states, since actual distribution varies with the car as specified.

      Aim 50:50 front-to-rear weight balance (Toyota’s stated target)

    4. 04

      An active rear differential

      Between the rear wheels sits a differential controlled by an electric motor acting on multiplate clutches. It can share torque between the two driven wheels while cornering, under power and under braking, and Toyota describes its locking as stepless, anywhere from fully open to fully locked.

      Rear axle Electronically controlled active differential, 0–100% locking

    5. 05

      Brakes and tyres

      Four-piston fixed callipers grip 348 mm ventilated discs at the front, with single-piston floating callipers on 345 mm discs behind. The 19-inch wheels carry Michelin Pilot Super Sport tyres, 255/35 at the front and wider 275/35 at the rear, so the driven axle gets the larger contact patch.

      Brakes 348 × 36 mm front, 345 × 24 mm rear; 255/35 and 275/35 ZR19

    05Driving character

    A short wheelbase and a wide track. The rear wheels in charge.

    The Supra is a two-seat, front-engined, rear-drive coupé, and nearly everything about its layout points one way. The engine is an inline-six in the nose, the drive goes only to the rear wheels, and the car is short between its axles but wide across them. It is a layout usually chosen for balance, and balance is the quality Toyota names first.

    The active differential is the part that does the fine work. By varying how tightly it couples the rear wheels, it can keep the car from pushing wide on the way into a corner or stepping out as the power goes on. Behind it the suspension is a five-link axle, and the steering is electric rack and pinion at 15.1 to 1. What that adds up to behind the wheel is a matter of the driver’s own judgement, not something the figures on this page can show.

    At 1,495 kg (automatic, without driver) it weighs about 250 kg less than the GT-R on the previous page, and the eight-speed automatic and six-speed manual give two quite different ways to use the same 500 Nm. The manual is quoted as 0.3 seconds slower to 100 km/h, a gap that most drivers who pick it will not mind.

    The 3D scene above uses a third-party model, credited in the page footer. It is a stand-in rather than a scan: it is longer and noticeably broader for its height than the published dimensions, and its wheels, trim and details are not matched to a specific model year.

    07Closing

    Seventeen years away. Six cylinders, two seats, rear drive.

    The A80 left production in 2002 and the A90 arrived in 2019. In between, the Supra name waited. The car that carries it now is the smallest on this site: a compact coupé on a 2,470 mm wheelbase, with a turbocharged inline-six in front and a differential that decides, wheel by wheel, where the power goes.
