    Dossier Nº 03 / 05GT-R R35

    It stopped being a Skyline. It did not stop being a GT-R.

    A twin-turbo V6 built by hand, a gearbox moved to the rear axle and four driven wheels. The R35 kept a nameplate from 1969 and gave it a new car to carry it. This page covers the 2017-onward car, in its European specification.

    The first Skyline GT-R arrived in 1969, a four-door sports saloon. The name returned on later generations and went quiet when the R34 ended production in 2002. Five years passed without a GT-R. Then, in 2007, Nissan unveiled the R35, and for the first time the car was no longer called a Skyline.

    The R35 paired a 3.8-litre twin-turbo V6 with a dual-clutch gearbox and all-wheel drive, and then spent eighteen years being refined rather than replaced. Output rose in steps from 353 kW (480 PS) at launch to the 419 kW (570 PS) of the car shown here.

    The largest update came for the 2017 model year, which Nissan called the most significant change since 2007: a new face with the V-motion grille, a stiffer bonnet, revised aerodynamics, and a calmer, smoother drivetrain. The last R35 was built at Nissan’s Tochigi plant on 26 August 2025, a Premium Edition T-spec in Midnight Purple for a customer in Japan.

    Read on in the order the car is built: the engine, the numbers, the shape, the drive, then the sheet.

    02VR38DETT engine and performance

    One technician, one engine, signed.

    The engine is called the VR38DETT: a 3.8-litre V6 with its cylinders in two banks of three, set at 60 degrees, with an aluminium block and heads, dual overhead camshafts and 24 valves, fed by two turbochargers. It sits at the front of the car, in line with it.

    Each engine is assembled by hand, from start to finish, by a single specialist technician called a takumi, who signs it with a plaque. In the car on this page it makes 419 kW (570 PS) at 6,800 rpm and 637 Nm from 3,300 to 5,800 rpm, which means the torque is available across most of the useful range rather than at one narrow peak.

    Power goes to a six-speed dual-clutch gearbox that is not behind the engine but at the rear axle, and from there to all four wheels. Nissan-quoted figures for the 2017 car are 2.7 seconds from 0 to 100 km/h and 315 km/h flat out. Some listings give 2.8 seconds for the sprint, so treat it as a quoted figure.

    04Exterior and engineering

    A shape that works for a living.

    Nissan described the 2017 changes as aimed at cooling, downforce and stability. Five details, each with a job.

    1. 01The V-motion grille
    2. 02A stiffer bonnet
    3. 03Sills and bumpers
    4. 04Brakes in the wheels
    5. 05Four seats, one body
    1. 01

      The V-motion grille

      For 2017 the GT-R took Nissan’s V-motion grille, in a matte chrome finish with a mesh pattern, and made it larger than before. The reason is air: more of it reaches the engine and the systems around it, and Nissan says it does so without adding drag. The bumper below it was redrawn, and daytime running lights were added.

      Front Enlarged V-motion grille, redesigned bumper, daytime running lights

    2. 02

      A bonnet that holds its shape

      The 2017 bonnet is stiffer, with more pronounced character lines flowing into the new bumper and front lip. Nissan’s stated reason is practical: a reinforced bonnet is less prone to deforming at speed, which helps the car’s aerodynamics at the top end.

      Bonnet Reinforced against deformation at speed

    3. 03

      Sills and bumpers that manage the air

      Along the sides, revised sills channel air around the body instead of beneath it, which reduces lift. At the back the bumper was reshaped as part of the same package. Nissan said nearly all the visual changes were meant to improve aerodynamics, aimed at downforce, lower drag and stability at speed.

      Aerodynamics Revised sills and bumpers; lift reduced, stability improved

    4. 04

      Brakes that fill the wheels

      Behind the 20-inch wheels sit Brembo monobloc callipers, six-piston at the front and four-piston at the rear, gripping large ventilated floating discs. A car of about 1,750 kg that reaches 315 km/h needs brakes of that size.

      Brakes Brembo monobloc, six-piston front, four-piston rear

    5. 05

      Four seats, one body

      The GT-R is a coupé with a rear seat. It is 4,710 mm long on a 2,780 mm wheelbase, 1,895 mm wide, and carries 315 litres of luggage. Nissan’s own description of the car is a performance four-seater.

      Layout Four seats, 315 L luggage space

    05Driving character and all-wheel drive

    Rear-drive by habit. All-wheel drive when it matters.

    The GT-R’s drivetrain is unusual in where it puts things. The engine is up front, but the six-speed dual-clutch gearbox is built into a transaxle beside the rear differential, joined to the engine by a shaft. That moves weight rearward and helps the balance, at the price of a very complicated driveline.

    The all-wheel-drive system is called ATTESA E-TS. In normal driving it sends all the drive to the rear wheels, a split of 0:100 front to rear, so the car behaves like a rear-drive one. When the situation calls for it, it can send up to half the torque to the front. Nissan lists the inputs it watches: speed, lateral acceleration, steering angle, wheel slip and the road surface.

    The result is a car that can put a great deal of power down early in a corner, on a wet road as well as a dry one. The 2017 update added refinement to that: softer damper settings, smoother gearbox shifts and revised steering, aimed at making the car easier to live with over a long drive without removing its edge.

    At 1,752 kg it is not a light car, and it does not pretend to be. What it offers instead is composure: a heavy, fast, four-wheel-driven coupé that treats a slippery road as an ordinary day.

    07Closing

    Eighteen years, one idea. Built by hand, driven by four wheels.

    The R35 is a record of an engineering argument made repeatedly and refined patiently: a hand-built twin-turbo V6, a gearbox at the back, and a drive system that decides for itself where the grip is. Nissan has said the GT-R name will return. This is the car that carried it for eighteen years.
